# License and adoption

When rights matter, distinguish a promising research lead from a candidate
whose terms have been checked for the intended use. Registry or repository
metadata is useful initial evidence; mention material uncertainty or
contradictions instead of treating a label as a complete assessment.

Before adopting third-party code or assets, check the applicable license and
material obligations against the project's policy and intended use. Consider
whether we are copying code, depending on a package, distributing a product,
or calling a separate service. Follow project requirements for version pins,
notices, records, and further review; immutable links or artifact digests are
useful when reproducibility or project policy calls for them, not a universal
requirement. Service access terms are separate from the license of any client
code or assets.

Compatibility depends on the actual use: do not treat all copyleft as
incompatible. For example, GPL code may fit a GPL project, and MPL or LGPL
dependencies raise different questions from copied source. If a material
question remains unresolved, say what is uncertain and avoid presenting
direct reuse as established. A candidate that cannot be adopted may still
provide ideas or references, subject to its terms.
