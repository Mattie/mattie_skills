# Soft Skill Creator

Create skills that teach a sensibility. Most skills succeed by being followed; these succeed by being inhabited — writing, voice, creativity, observation, sensory judgment, style, feel, and whatever neighboring territory (technical included) runs on qualitative judgment.

The difference from ordinary skill creation is a difference in degree of procedural rigidity:

```plaintext
default skill creator: LEAST [-------*--] MOST
soft-skill-creator:    LEAST [--*-------] MOST
```

This position still permits general procedures, useful structure, clarity, and focused guidelines, with constraints where needed. Increase rigidity a little where variation creates a concrete problem.

With this skill, err on the side of vibe, art, style, and feel; consciously correct for the reasoning model's over-rational leanings that turn every skill into a bureaucratic scientific process.

## Principles

**Words carry posture, not just information.** In a soft skill, a modifier can be a permission, a repetition can be a rhythm, and the "general" in "general procedures" can be doing quiet load-bearing work — it softens "procedures" into a broad way of proceeding with interpretive room. Before trimming anything, ask what tone, permission, or degree of rigidity the word may be carrying. Redundant as information is not the same as redundant as feel.

**Feel-bearing material is content.** Metaphors, rhythm, contrasts, worked examples, small drawings like the scale above — these are not decoration awaiting cleanup. They are how a sensibility gets transmitted efficiently. Keep them when they help the model inhabit the intended feel; cut them only when they genuinely mislead or crowd out something better.

**Demonstration over specification.** A sample passage, a metaphor, or a before-and-after pair usually teaches a sensibility better than the rule it implies. When drafting, prefer showing. Compare:

> Evaluate composition using the rule of thirds. Verify the subject occupies an intersection point. Confirm negative space does not exceed 40% of the frame.

against:

> Let the frame breathe. If your own eye snags somewhere, the viewer's will too.

Both are guidance about the same thing. Only one of them can be inhabited.

**Room is the resting state.** Ordinary skill creation grants interpretive room as an exception — when multiple approaches are reasonable. Here the spring is mounted the other way: room is where a soft skill rests, and rigidity is what you add, a little, locally, where variation causes a concrete problem. A soft skill may absolutely contain a precise constraint, a fixed sequence, even a script; it just has to earn its place with a real problem it prevents.

**Watch for the over-rational pull.** A reasoning model drafting or editing a qualitative skill will reach, unprompted, for numbered steps, evaluation criteria, taxonomies of cases, and measurable checkpoints. Sometimes one of those genuinely helps. Usually it is the model soothing itself. When you feel a draft hardening, reread it for the feel it was supposed to carry — and put the feel back.

## Drafting and editing

Write the skill in the voice it teaches, as far as the subject allows. A skill about warmth that reads cold undermines itself on contact.

The compression pressure of ordinary skill writing does not apply here. Let the entrypoint be as long as the sensibility needs — no target in either direction.

A soft skill still serves the user's actual request: keep their explicit choices, and don't turn one example, one past failure, or one personal taste into universal law unless they tell you it is one.

When softening an existing skill or revising a soft one, trims are the risky edits, not additions. For each proposed cut, name what the passage was carrying; if the answer is tone, permission, or rhythm, the cut needs a better reason than length.

## Validation

The mechanical checks are unchanged: frontmatter with name and description, lowercase-hyphen naming, no leftover scaffolding. The real validation is a judged reading — use the skill on a realistic request and read what comes out. Does it sound like the intended sensibility, or like a process document wearing its clothes? Read it aloud if that helps; the ear catches what a checklist can't. Avoid tests that pattern-match wording: output that produced the "right" phrases in the wrong voice has failed.

## Mechanics

The anatomy is the same as any skill: a folder named after the skill holding `SKILL.md` (frontmatter `name` and `description`; body loaded on use), with optional `agents/openai.yaml`, `references/`, `scripts/`, and `assets/` when the work genuinely needs them. Soft skills usually stay self-contained — one voice in one file. Split into references when a genuinely distinct mode needs its own guide, not to make the entrypoint look tidy.

Write the description in the same key as the skill itself: say what it's for and when it applies. A boundary is welcome when it prevents likely misrouting, but don't fence the territory too precisely — soft skills often apply in adjacent technical domains whenever qualitative judgment leads.
